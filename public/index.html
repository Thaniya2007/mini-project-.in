Hospital Safety & Public Health

Why Safe Hospital Waste Management Matters

Biomedical waste contains potentially hazardous pathogens and chemical agents. Unscientific segregation and improper disposal pose severe biological risks to healthcare personnel, sanitation workers, patients, and the community.

Infection & Disease Control

Eliminates hospital-acquired infections (HAI) and prevents the spread of viral pathogens including Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, HIV, and multidrug-resistant bacteria.

Needle-Stick Injury Prevention

Translucent, puncture-proof sharps bins protect nurses, doctors, and sanitation handlers from accidental needle-sticks, surgical cuts, and contamination.

Environmental Protection

Prevents soil, groundwater, and municipal water contamination. Non-chlorinated bags avoid toxic dioxin and furan emission during high-temp treatment.

Regulatory Compliance

Complies with Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and WHO Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules with digital barcodes, manifests, and tamper-proof GPS trails.

Biomedical Classification

Four Colour-Coded Waste Segregation Bins

Healthcare staff must segregate waste at the point of generation. Each color corresponds to specific treatment and disposal protocols.

Infectious Bio-Waste Photo
YELLOW

1. Yellow Bin

Infectious & Pathological Waste
Incineration

Highly contaminated waste, anatomical items, tissues, blood-saturated dressings, laboratory cultures, and expired drugs.

Included Examples:
  • Human anatomical waste (tissues, organs, body parts, placenta)
  • Blood-contaminated cotton, gauze, dressings, plaster casts
  • Expired medicines, cytotoxic pharmaceuticals, discard vaccines
  • Microbiology, biotechnology & laboratory culture specimens
Rule: Non-chlorinated yellow plastic bags CBWTF Incinerator / Pyrolysis
Contaminated Plastics Photo
RED

2. Red Bin

Contaminated Recyclable Plastics
Autoclave & Shred

Recyclable plastic consumables contaminated with patient bodily fluids, requiring steam sterilization before shredding.

Included Examples:
  • Contaminated disposable examination & surgical gloves
  • Intravenous (IV) fluid sets, tubing, and bottles
  • Urinary bags, drainage tubes, suction catheters
  • Syringes without needles, vacutainers, dialysis tubing
Rule: Red non-chlorinated plastic bags Autoclaving / Authorized Recyclers
Sharps & Needles Photo
WHITE

3. White Translucent Bin

Sharps & Contaminated Metals
Puncture-Proof

Cut or puncture hazard objects capable of transmitting bloodborne infections. Strictly stored in rigid, puncture-proof containers.

Included Examples:
  • Hypodermic needles, scalpels, surgical disposable blades
  • Suture needles, lancets, biopsy needles, trocars
  • Contaminated sharp stainless steel instruments
  • Broken contaminated glass ampoule tips with sharp edges
Rule: Never recap or bend needles Autoclaving & Metal Encapsulation
Glassware & Implants Photo
BLUE

4. Blue Bin / Box

Glassware & Metallic Implants
Disinfection

Glass containers, medicine vials, and metallic orthopedic implants that require chemical disinfection before secondary recycling.

Included Examples:
  • Medicine glass bottles, ampoules, vials (excluding cytotoxic)
  • Glass slides, microscope cover slips, specimen test tubes
  • Metallic orthopedic implants, pins, screws, plates
  • Dental metallic prosthetics & contaminated alloy fragments
Rule: Puncture-resistant cardboard box / blue container Sodium Hypochlorite Treatment
♻️
Separate General Municipal Waste (Black / Green Bins)

Food waste, fruit peels, leftover meals (Green Bin) and dry paper, office stationery, clean packaging carton (Black Bin) must NEVER be mixed with biomedical waste.

Digital Chain of Custody

Eight-Stage Collection Lifecycle

From hospital ward pickup to the Central Bio-Medical Waste Treatment Facility (CBWTF), every step is digitally stamped, photograph-verified, and tracked via GPS coordinates.

1 Hospital Staff

Request Submitted

Photo Verification First: Clinical staff uploads or captures a waste photograph first. The visual system verifies the items and unlocks the appropriate colour-coded bin for safe segregation.

2 Dispatch System

Request Accepted

System dispatch acknowledges the collection request and broadcasts pickup alert to active bio-waste collectors in the hospital zone.

3 Collector

Collector Assigned

Authorized waste collector accepts the pickup job. Assigned vehicle number and collector contact details appear on the department dashboard.

4 In Transit

Collection in Progress

Collector reaches the department floor with specialized safety cart, verifies color code tag, and initiates barcode scan.

5 Verification

Waste Collected

Collector measures verified weight on calibrated scale, uploads photographic proof of collection, and seals the container.

6 GPS Vehicle

Transported

Bio-waste is loaded into specialized GPS-monitored vehicle. Telemetry streams live transit coordinates on the monitoring map.

7 CBWTF Handover

Handed Over to Facility

Waste delivered to authorized CBWTF facility. Facility supervisor verifies electronic manifest and logs physical arrival.

8 Certified

Completed

Final treatment certificate generated. Audit logs, Excel and PDF manifests are permanently sealed for pollution control inspection.
